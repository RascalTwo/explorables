Copilot Brand Kits, without the jargon

What a brand is · what a Brand Kit turns it into · and which rules Copilot will actually hold the line on

A Brand Kit is your brand guidelines, converted into something a machine can apply without judgement. The catch is that only some of it converts. Below are seventeen brand rules a real organisation would have. Units, and an honest caveat. “Enforcement” is how reliably Copilot reproduces that rule unprompted, 0–100. “Cost if wrong” is what it costs the organisation when it doesn’t, 0–100. These are calibrated judgement, not measurement — Microsoft publishes no such numbers. The ordering is the claim, and it follows directly from documented mechanism: a rule stored as a file is applied deterministically, a rule stored as prose is interpreted by a model. 02Where a Brand Kit actually reaches

A Brand Kit governs what Copilot generates — and nothing else. Within that, coverage is uneven enough that “we set up a Brand Kit” and “our output is on-brand” are very different claims.

Outside the kit’s reach entirely — three things it is not for
  • Anyone working without Copilot. Someone who opens PowerPoint and types is governed by your template and the Organization Asset Library, not by the kit. Put the same .potx in both so the two paths agree.
  • How your SharePoint intranet looks. That is the SharePoint / Microsoft 365 Brand Center — a separate feature, set up separately, that predates Copilot. The two are converging but are not the same store.
  • Anything outside Microsoft 365. Your website, your print, your email platform, your design tools. The kit has no reach there at all.

04Who is allowed to touch it

Two separate policies in config.office.com govern brand kits, and they AND together. Row three is the trap: putting someone in the brand-manager group but not the creation group locks them out entirely.

In “Restrict brand kit creation” groupIn “Elevated role for Brand Managers” groupWhat they can actually do
NoNoConsume official kits only — can’t create or edit
YesNoPersonal kits only — can’t publish anything official
NoYesNothing. Can’t create or edit at all — the trap
YesYesFull brand manager — creates and publishes official kits

Both policies take up to 24 hours to take effect. The Elevated-role policy additionally must sit on a tenant-scoped configuration at priority 0, or it silently does nothing.

05What to actually go and collect

  • The current brand guidelines PDF. One document, PDF only — Copilot parses it to extract palette, voice, logo and typography rules. Ask who owns it and when it was last revised.
  • The approved PowerPoint template as .potx, authored in PowerPoint for Windows or Mac (Web-authored templates aren’t supported), ideally with sample slides showing each layout in use — that’s how Copilot learns which layout to pick.
  • Every approved logo variant, and the photo library — tagged. Filename, description and keywords are what drive image retrieval accuracy.
  • A named brand manager. This is a governance decision, not an IT one.
  • A human review step for everything in the red zone above. That is the whole finding.

Setup is roughly an afternoon of configuration. Agreeing on which blue is the real blue is the actual project.
