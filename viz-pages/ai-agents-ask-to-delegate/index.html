What Is an AI Agent?

AI is sliding from a thing you ask to a thing you delegate to — and you don't have to be an engineer to use it, or even build one. Five short scenes, plain language, with examples for both the engineer and everyone else.
01

Chatbot or agent? It's a slide, not a switch.

Everyone says "AI agent" now, but most "AI" is still just a chatbot. The difference is simple: a chatbot talks; an agent goes and does. Between those two ends is a smooth slide.

◀ just talksgoes and does ▶
The one line to remember: if it can only hand you words, it's a chatbot — no matter how smart it sounds. The moment it can do things in the real world (look something up, send an email, place an order) and keep going until the job's done, you've crossed into agent territory.

04

You don't have to be an engineer.

Building one of these used to mean writing software. Not anymore. There's a line between what still needs an engineer and what anyone can now do — and that line keeps sliding toward everyone with every release.

✓ Anyone can do this today — describe, connect, schedule, share

▼ The line creeps down every release. A year ago, "plug in a new data source" or "put it on a schedule" needed an engineer. Today it's a button.

Both land on a shared shelf anyone can reach. The non-engineer snaps the engineer's catalog plug into their assistant; the engineer drops the recap assistant into a bigger automation. What's built at one bench works at the other.

Wait — how do the benches feed each other?

Through a standard plug. The same way any USB device fits any USB port, there's now a standard way to plug an AI capability into any AI tool. Build it to that standard once, and every tool — a chat assistant, a coworker's app, an engineer's coding tool, a vendor's product — can use it without custom wiring.

You'll hear the techy names for these standards. They just mean "the USB for AI":

MCP — plug a tool or data source into an AI A2A — let one AI hand work to another

Same result, different parts. Non-engineers wire together ready-made building blocks; engineers manufacture the blocks. And more of those blocks land on everyone's bench every month.

05

Build it once — share it everywhere.

That standard plug is what makes it all scale. Build a useful capability once — say, "look up anything in our product catalog" — and you can expose it at whatever reach you choose: your team, your whole organization, or the entire world. Build once, plug in everywhere.

06

Who's actually shipping this — the big players.

None of this is hypothetical. Every major player now ships the same two things: a no-code "make your own assistant" for everyone, and a more powerful builder platform for the engineers. Same pattern, different brand names.
