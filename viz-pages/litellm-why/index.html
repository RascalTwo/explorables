Why LiteLLM?

One front door for every model — what it solves, what you get, and how to get access.

What it solves

Without a gateway, every app wires straight to every model provider. That mesh is where the pain lives: keys copied into each app, no shared budget or audit, and swapping a model means editing every caller. LiteLLM collapses the mesh into one OpenAI-compatible front door.

Without a gateway

N apps × M providers

  • Keys live in every app — no central rotation or revocation
  • No shared view of spend, limits, or who called what
  • Swap a model → touch every caller
With LiteLLM

One gateway, one endpoint

  • One place for keys, budgets, routing, and logging
  • Apps speak one OpenAI-compatible /v1 API
  • Swap or fail over models centrally — apps don't change

What you get

The capabilities the gateway adds on top of raw model access. The full PoC proves each of these end-to-end — see the deep dive below.

How do you get access?

Two paths, depending on whether your app can authenticate with the identity provider.

Preferred

Bring your own OAuth

If your app already authenticates with the IdP, LiteLLM acts as an OAuth2 resource server: present your JWT, it maps to your client_id and enforces your limits. No key to mint, store, or rotate.

See how it works If you can't

Get vended a virtual key

Can't do OAuth? A self-service key-vendor mints, scopes, and rotates a virtual key for you — declaratively, via Ansible, Terraform, or an always-on Kubernetes operator.

Bring your own OAuth

LiteLLM acts as an OAuth2 resource server. Your existing identity-provider token is the credential — there's no API key to mint, store, or rotate.

What you do (one-time setup)
01Register your app

Create a client in the IdP and get its credentials (client_id + secret).

02Gateway trusts your IdP

Platform team points LiteLLM at the IdP's JWKS and maps a token claim → your identity. Done once.

03Call with a token

Your app fetches a token and sends it as a Bearer header. No key to manage.

How a call works

The gateway caches the IdP's public keys (JWKS) to verify tokens locally — no IdP round-trip per request. Tokens are short-lived, so access falls away automatically on expiry.
