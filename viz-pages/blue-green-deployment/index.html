The catch · what it costs you

It is not free, and it is not magic

You're paying for two

For as long as both copies are up, you're renting two of everything. On a small site that's lunch money. On a big one it's a real line item — the premium you pay for the insurance.

The filing cabinet isn't copied

Orders, accounts, messages — both copies read and write the same records. So the new version has to cope with the old version's paperwork, and the old one has to cope with the new version's, in case you flip back. This is the part that actually takes planning.

Someone was mid-checkout

Flipping the front door redirects people arriving; it doesn't teleport the ones already inside. Whoever was halfway through something may have to start again, unless the two copies are built to hand that over.

Your test isn't a real crowd

A few staff clicking around is not a Tuesday lunchtime rush. Some problems only appear under real traffic — which is exactly why the one-second flip back matters more than the testing did.

So: blue-green isn't a way to stop making mistakes. It's a way to make mistakes cheap to undo — trading a bit of rent for the ability to change your mind in a second instead of an hour.
